01

Definition of Corporate Governance

Corporate governance refers to the framework of rules, systems, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. It involves balancing the interests of various stakeholders such as shareholders, management, employees, customers, regulators, and the community to ensure ethical business practices, accountability, and long-term sustainability.

Decision-making Processes

Board of directors and executive management guide organizational decisions.

Regulatory Compliance

Organizations follow applicable laws, regulations and industry standards.

Transparency and Disclosure

Financial reporting and corporate policies are communicated clearly.

Stakeholder Rights Protection

Shareholders, employees and creditors receive fair treatment and protection.

02

Importance of Corporate Governance

Enhancing Investor Confidence

Transparent and accountable governance attracts investors, improves access to capital and reduces risks of fraud and financial mismanagement.

Ensuring Legal and Ethical Compliance

Governance helps companies adhere to laws and promotes ethical behavior, preventing corruption and insider trading.

Improving Operational Efficiency

Clear roles for the board and management reduce conflicts, improve decision-making and encourage long-term strategic planning.

Protecting Stakeholder Interests

Strong governance ensures fair treatment of shareholders and safeguards employees, customers and creditors.

Boosting Corporate Reputation and Sustainability

Strong governance improves brand value and market credibility while supporting Environmental, Social and Governance commitments.

03

Objectives of Corporate Governance

01

Accountability and Transparency

Ensure management and the board are answerable to shareholders and disclose accurate financial and operational information.

02

Fair Treatment of Shareholders

Protect minority shareholders and ensure equitable dividend distribution and voting rights.

03

Risk Management and Compliance

Identify and mitigate financial, operational and reputational risks while complying with legal requirements.

04

Sustainable Business Growth

Promote long-term profitability and align business strategies with stakeholder interests.

05

Ethical Leadership and CSR

Encourage responsible decision-making and integrate social and environmental concerns into business operations.

Conclusion

Why Governance Matters

Corporate governance is vital for maintaining trust, ensuring compliance, and driving sustainable business success. By adhering to strong governance principles, organizations can enhance performance, reduce risks and create value for all stakeholders.

04

Key Principles of Corporate Governance

01

Accountability

Companies must be accountable to shareholders and stakeholders for decisions and performance, with clear roles and responsibilities.

02

Transparency

Provide timely and accurate disclosure of financial performance, risks and governance policies.

03

Fairness

Ensure equitable treatment of all shareholders and protect shareholder rights.

04

Responsibility

Promote ethical and sustainable business practices and compliance with laws and responsibilities.

05

Independence

Independent directors provide unbiased oversight and help avoid excessive power concentration.

06

Risk Management

Use effective internal controls and proactively identify and mitigate financial, operational and reputational risks.

07

Board Effectiveness

A diverse and skilled board should have appropriate expertise and regular performance evaluations.

08

Stakeholder Engagement

Recognize employees, customers, suppliers and community interests while balancing profit and sustainability.

05

Structure of Corporate Governance

Board of Directors

Composition and Roles

The mix of executive, non-executive and independent directors oversee company strategy, monitor management and protect shareholder interests.

Role of Independent Directors

Directors with no ties to the company provide unbiased oversight, especially in conflicts of interest.

Committees of the Board

Audit Committee

Ensures accurate financial reporting, compliance and effective risk management.

Compensation Committee

Determines executive pay to align leadership incentives with company performance.

Nominating and Governance Committee

Selects qualified board candidates and establishes governance policies.

Shareholders

Rights of Shareholders

Shareholders may vote on major decisions, receive dividends, access company information and seek legal recourse for mismanagement.

Shareholder Communication

Annual meetings, reports and investor calls help maintain transparency and accountability.
